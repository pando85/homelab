    battery-optimizer

    Two workloads of pando85/home-energy-optimizer, deployed from apps/battery-optimizer/ with one image, one PVC and one ConfigMap:

    • pipeline: a CronJob that extracts Home Assistant recorder history and Prometheus samples into an append-only Parquet store, validates it, and builds the 15-minute analysis dataset (Phase B).
    • service: the resident Phase C shadow service (battery-optimizer serve). It reads Home Assistant and Prometheus, solves the MPC plan every 15 minutes and records shadow intents and a shadow-vs-native comparison. In shadow (deployed) it has no actuation path and no write credentials; dry_run and live add the battery actuator of ADR-0007, see Live battery control. The production plan and its history live in planning/energy-baseline-production-execution.md.

    The application code, ADRs and domain docs live in the app repository; this page covers only the deployment.

    Design notes and gotchas

    • Why daily with an 8 day HA window. HA's recorder keeps 10 days (purge_keep_days default, nothing overrides it). Rows older than that are gone for good, so the binding rule is "never more than 10 days between two successful runs". Daily with an 8 day lookback tolerates 7 missed runs. The window is deliberately shorter than the retention: the recorder purges nightly, so the oldest chunk of a 10 day window is partly or wholly gone and would raise a coverage gap every night (see the next note). Re-pulling is free because the HA extractor dedups against rows already in the store. Chunk width stays at the CLI default (86400 s); changing it on a live store mints new carry-in anchors.
    • Prometheus extraction is not deduplicated across runs, so the lookback is only 3 days (about 40 KB per day-part). The Prometheus archive has no retention limit, so there is no urgency.
    • Bootstrap. If /data/manifests holds no finished Prometheus extraction (empty PVC), the run extracts Prometheus from PROM_BOOTSTRAP_START (2024-01-01) instead of the 3 day lookback. A manifest is written only by a finished run, so an interrupted bootstrap retries from scratch.
    • Build cadence and window. build is content-addressed and write-once, and measured on the cluster every new extraction changes the dataset id (the part list changes), adding about 4.2 MB per run (~1.5 GB a year if built daily) while nothing consumes the dataset yet. The job therefore builds only on UTC days 01 and 02 of the month (BUILD_ON_DAYS), over the complete months [BUILD_START, first of this month); other days log build skipped. The newest partial month is not in the dataset until the month closes. There is no CLI-side garbage collection of superseded datasets. Run a manual build job if you need one off-cycle (steps 1-3 still run).
    • Permissions. No fsGroup (slow startup on openebs-zfspv). A fresh ZFS dataset is root:root 0755, so the fix-permissions init container chowns the volume root to 65532 (recursively only if the root is not already owned by 65532). See ci-runner-workspace-permission-denied.md.
    • Alerts use release: monitoring, not operator.prometheus.io/instance: prometheus-long-term. kube-state-metrics and kubelet series exist only in the main Prometheus; a rule labelled for prometheus-long-term would load but never fire.
    • No NetworkPolicy. An egress-only policy (DNS, HA 8123, long-term Prometheus 9090) ran from 0.2.1 until 0.3.0 and was removed on 2026-10-25: battery-optimizer is first-party software published from our own repository, and the repo keeps NetworkPolicies for workloads that are not under our control (LLM harnesses). Outside live its only credential is the read-only HA token and it has no actuation path; in live the HA client can send only the allowlisted Omnibattery writes (ADR-0007).
    • The two power signals come from a Gen1 Shelly EM over MQTT at 2 s; the counters still poll. shellyem-main (192.168.86.130) publishes shellies/shellyem-244CAB417668/emeter/{0,1}/power to mosquitto every 2 s (mqtt_update_period: 2) and HA consumes them via apps/home-assistant/config/integrations/shelly_em_mqtt.yaml. Since the 2026-10-27 cutover, grid_power and observed carry those IDs from the mqtt platform, measured as battery_optimizer_input_age_seconds{role="grid_power"} = 2.3 s (was 31.9 s). grid_energy and everything else still arrive over the native integration at 2.2 × 15 s = 33 s, which is correct for a monotonic counter consumed as 900 s deltas — a faster transport would add recorder rows without adding information. The device still carries a permanent error-level push_update_* repair, because CoIoT is multicast-only (peer: "") and nothing forwards it to the HA pod; that is a hygiene item, not a data problem. A faster transport initially looked unusable — the device reboot-looped — but that was mis-diagnosed: the loop needs MQTT and coiot.enabled: false and an HA setup_retry storm, so MQTT is safe as long as CoIoT stays enabled (planning/shelly-em-mqtt-implementation.md). Do not rename these entities or the device: friendly_name is a Prometheus label and part of the archive's series_key. Renaming also moves recorder history — if an ID must ever change hands again, use remove-then-promote (home-assistant-entity-id-handover.md).
    • Never disable CoIoT on that device, and treat HA restarts as a checked operation. With coiot.enabled: false the firmware stops serving /cit/*, HA's initialize() hangs on GET /cit/d, and the next HA restart drops all 14 shellyem-main entities into setup_retry. That happened on 2026-10-26 and cost ~48 min of history (18:02:35Z-18:50:34Z). Since the MQTT cutover the blast radius is narrower but not gone: grid_power and observed would keep working (they come from the broker), while grid_energy — what the dataset is actually built from — would be lost, and MQTT being permanently enabled means the reboot-loop co-factor is always present. Before any HA ConfigMap change (Reloader restarts the pod), verify GET /cit/d still answers 200. Recovery, correct endpoint and pitfalls: shelly-gen1-coiot-disabled-breaks-ha-setup.md.
    • grid_energy still takes ~20 poll timeouts a day; grid_power no longer does. The native /status REST poll occasionally exceeds DEVICE_IO_TIMEOUT = 10 s (~19.7 unavailable rows/day on metadata_id 182 over a 10-day window, dating back before MQTT existed), which flips the whole block to unavailable at T + 33 s + 10 s. In the Prometheus archive those become missing 60 s samples — the 2026-10-27 03:30Z run saw 4420 of ~4526 expected over 3 days and still reported violations=0. Watch for it if the coverage check below ever starts failing: the fix is to move the counters to MQTT too, which has so far not been worth the recorder cost.
    • tariff.yaml and service.yaml are vendored copies of config/ in the app repository (the image carries no config). Update both together; the tariff config hash is part of every dataset id. The CronJob mounts the same ConfigMap at /config and sets BATTERY_OPTIMIZER_SERVICE_CONFIG=/config/service.yaml, so build and extract read battery.commissioned_at and the battery counters from the same service.yaml as the service; set them there on installation day and nothing else needs changing.
    • Coverage gaps fail the Job. From 0.2.1 extract exits 3 when the run's manifest records a coverage gap (HA: empty_chunk, empty_chunk_after_window_filter, chunk_accounting_mismatch, no_rows_for_entity, no_rows_extracted:all; Prometheus: no_samples_extracted, no_series_chunks, series_without_samples_chunks). The data and manifest are kept, but the Job fails and BatteryOptimizerJobFailed fires, so a silent gap is no longer silent. The pipeline does not pass --allow-coverage-gaps (except for the one-off Prometheus bootstrap, whose 2024 history has known holes). If a window legitimately has no data, fix the window rather than opting out.

    Dashboards per component

    One dashboard per component KPI table of the app repository (docs/components/*.md, section "KPIs"). They share the ${datasource} variable (default prometheus-long-term), the tags battery-optimizer, energy and battery, and a dashboard-links block that lists every dashboard tagged battery, so the six battery dashboards link to each other (a new battery dashboard needs only the tag). Panel descriptions state each sign and every approximation.

    Component Dashboard (uid) What it answers
    battery-optimizer Battery: plan vs actual (battery-plan-vs-actual), Battery optimizer service (battery-optimizer-shadow) day-ahead against final plan against actual; readiness, solves, shadow-vs-native cost
    battery-load-forecast Battery: load forecast (battery-load-forecast) forecast error of the two plans
    battery-model Battery: model and actuation (battery-model-actuation) commanded, planned and actual power (+ charge), clamped power and energy not delivered, SOC tracking error at the slot boundary, daily discharge/charge ratio. The commanded power is derived (charge: PD target - house load, since the PD target is a grid-import setpoint; discharge: - system discharge cap, a ceiling). There is no round-trip-efficiency metric: the panel is a discharge/charge ratio, and an "RTE estimate" line is drawn only while the SOC moved 3 points or less over the 24 h
    battery-thermal Battery: temperature (battery-temperature) measured against predicted temperature, derate factor and state, Omnibattery's limit settings, predicted achievable against actual charge power, MAE, RMSE and bias per lead (final, 1h, 3h), model status, parameters, last fit, refits and predictions by result; the HA sensor sensor.venus_internal_temperature and the hours above 35, 40 and 45 degC. Needs planning.thermal.mode: shadow (service 0.10.0); the error panels fill as slots close, the last-fit stat and the derate-error panels stay empty until a refit is accepted and a derate slot has closed
    tariff-prices Tariff prices (battery-tariff-prices) next-day publication (state, last flip time, hour per day), price and plan horizon, day-ahead lead, the PVPC price from sensor.esios_pvpc (current hour only; the forward curve is a sensor attribute, not a metric), daily cost native against counterfactual and their difference

    The cost panels are delta() sums over the per-process-run gauges shadow_native_cost_eur and shadow_counterfactual_cost_eur (they restart at 0 with every pod, so a restart makes the day approximate). Since the battery is commissioned, native is the house with the battery as it ran and counterfactual the replayed shadow decisions: the pair is not a house with and without the battery. shadow has no actuator, so the actuator panels need dry_run or live.

    Size. The ConfigMap holds all thirteen JSON files (about 460 KB). Client-side apply stores the whole object in the last-applied-configuration annotation, which is limited to 256 KiB, so the ConfigMap in templates/dashboards.yaml carries argocd.argoproj.io/sync-options: ServerSideApply=true (as kanidm, pyroscope and tempo do). The object limit is 1 MiB, so there is room for roughly the same again; past about 800 KB split the climate files into a second ConfigMap dashboards-climate (a second template with the same label and ServerSideApply, its own file glob and target-directory annotation).

    Climate dashboards per component

    The climate KPI tables of the app repository (docs/components/*.md, section "KPIs") follow the same pattern: one dashboard per component that has at least three meaningful KPI panels, shared dashboards with one titled row per component where it does not. All carry the tags battery-optimizer, energy and climate, the ${datasource} variable and a dashboard-links block "Climate dashboards" (every dashboard tagged climate, so a new climate dashboard needs only the tag; Battery optimizer climate shadow carries it too and keeps its own panels). A panel description states what is approximated. Where the headline KPI has no metric yet, the panel shows the measured side or a baseline and the closing text panel "Not yet measured" names the gap.

    Component Dashboard (uid) What it answers
    climate-mpc Climate: comfort MPC (climate-mpc) plan production (runs by result, planned share), solver health (solves by status, not-optimal share, duration), top-offset share, degree-hours below 21 degC and the hall-kitchen against 23.5 degC as the rule delivered them
    thermal-model Climate: thermal model (climate-thermal-model) room-mean RMSE, skill and bias per lead (1, 6, 12, 24 h) and window against the preregistered limits, daily heat MdAPE, scored origins and valid weeks, measured room temperatures, gate status, refits
    cop-model Climate: COP model (climate-cop) the measured side: COP from the HeishaMon counters (24 h, 7 d, by mode), reported COP, lift in heat mode, starts and compressor hours (the d and e terms), heat and electricity per day. The model's own error is offline (hack/climate_cop.py)
    heat-pump-controller Climate: heat-pump controller (climate-heat-pump-controller) room temperature against setpoint and the section-B comfort criteria, rule activity (heating and hot-water hours against min_hours, starts, water target), kWh per heating degree-day, heater safety and tank cap
    outdoor-temperature Climate: outdoor temperature (climate-outdoor-temperature) the unit sensor and compressor frequency, trusted, unknown and duty shares from the compressor frequency, weather forecast archive fetches and age
    hp-consumption-forecast, boost-mode Climate: planned components (climate-planned), one row each planned, not built: the heat-pump load and a same-slot-yesterday baseline (MAE, bias) the forecast must beat; for boost the grid import against the 5.0 kW limit, battery SOC and charge power, tank maximum against 50 degC and the heater states

    Decisions: the first five have at least three KPI panels with real series (the outdoor-temperature ones are compressor-frequency approximations, and its forecast-error KPI is not measured). The two planned components have no metric of their own, only preconditions and baselines, so they share one dashboard with a titled row each instead of two near-empty ones. The thermal-model dashboard repeats the accuracy panels of Battery optimizer climate shadow on purpose (that one stays the job-level view) and adds the preregistered limits as dashed lines.

    Gaps (no metric exists): the MPC's planned comfort deficit and planned against actual heat-pump energy, the COP model's predicted COP and error, the outdoor temperature forecast error, the heat-pump consumption forecast error, boost activations and soak energy; the controller's decisions. The weather archive panels expose a real effect: while the weather.forecast_home entity does not answer, weather_forecast_fetches_total{result="failed"} grows and the MPC horizon holds the last reading.

    Day-ahead and final plan (0.9.0). The service keeps, per 15-minute slot, the day-ahead plan (the first plan that covered the slot, usually made the previous afternoon) next to the final plan (the intent frozen when the slot opened) and scores both against what happened. The metrics are battery_optimizer_slot_*{plan="day_ahead|final"}, load_forecast_*_total{plan}, plan_revision_kwh_total and shadow_day_ahead_energy_kwh_total; they appear after the first slot opens and closes, and the day-ahead values after a restart are rebuilt from the plan files on the PVC (startup log day-ahead rebuilt plan_files=N slots=M). Home Assistant gets four more template sensors from /plan current_slot in apps/home-assistant/config/integrations/battery_optimizer_plan.yaml: sensor.battery_optimizer_day_ahead_power and sensor.battery_optimizer_day_ahead_soc (next to the planned power and SOC, which the Battery dashboard calls Final plan), and sensor.battery_optimizer_day_ahead_load / sensor.battery_optimizer_final_load (forecast house load in W). They keep the existing sign (+ discharging, - charging), are unknown while the slot has no such plan and unavailable when the service does not answer (a service older than 0.9.0 has no current_slot, so they stay unknown). The Battery dashboard (dashboard-battery, storage mode) charts them as Day-ahead plan, Final plan and Actual (power, SOC and house load).

    Passive archives (0.3.0). The archive: block of service.yaml turns on two append-only collectors under /data/service/v1/: pvpc_snapshots/ (every state change of the PVPC price sensor, to measure when next-day prices really appear) and weather_forecasts/ (weather.get_forecasts for weather.forecast_home, hourly at HH:05 UTC). The weather query is the single call_service frame the HA guard lets through; every other service call is still refused in code. Watch battery_optimizer_pvpc_snapshots_total, battery_optimizer_weather_forecast_fetches_total{result} and battery_optimizer_archive_last_success_timestamp_seconds. There is no retention yet.

    The shadow store (/data/service/v1/) is private household data and is covered by the same PVC snapshots. Read the comparison with battery-optimizer shadow-report from a pod or a copy of the store; comparison_basis stays no_battery_pre_commissioning until battery telemetry is mapped, and that basis does not satisfy exit gate C.

    Climate shadow (track B2)

    From 0.4.0 the service also runs the climate shadow (climate_shadow: in files/service.yaml): the thermal model and the price-blind comfort MPC of the app repository on live data. Every hour at HH:10 UTC, on its own worker thread, it reads, predicts, plans, records and scores. It is read-only and actuates nothing: the plan is written to the PVC and never sent anywhere, it uses no new credential, and the HA guard still refuses every service call but weather.get_forecasts. App-side reference: docs/service.md#climate-shadow-track-b2 and the preregistration docs/planning/climate-preregistration-2026-27.md in the app repository.

    Input Source
    Room temperatures and setpoints (four thermostats), Aquarea outdoor sensor, outlet target, compressor frequency, heat / DHW / cool power consumed and produced, force-DHW switch, legacy AppDaemon enable and minimum hours, PVPC long-term Prometheus (hass_* series), 30 days fetched at the first run and then incrementally
    input_boolean.comfort_control_enable (regime), input_text.comfort_dhw_plan, input_boolean.comfort_away Home Assistant, through the existing read-only session; each may be absent. Until the comfort controller is deployed they do not exist and every record is regime legacy
    Outdoor forecast the service's own weather archive (weather_forecasts/, fetched at HH:05)

    Failure isolation. A missing prior, site file or config, a Prometheus error or a solver failure is logged (climate shadow not loaded: ..., climate shadow run failed), counted, written as a climate_shadow_error event and retried the next hour. It never changes readiness and never touches the battery planner, which keeps solving every 15 minutes. A healthy job logs nothing; check /status.

    ESPHome AC failsafe

    The battery holds its last charge or discharge command while Home Assistant is down (commissioning test: outages of 23 s to 10.5 minutes, no device timeout). The watchdog and the service's safe idle both need HA, so the battery's AC plug (Athom Smart Plug V2 "Battery", switch.smart_plug_v2_battery) carries a failsafe in its own device file. The shared packages/smart-plug-v2.yaml is unchanged:

    • after 5 minutes without a Home Assistant API client (api.connected with state_subscription_only, so a log viewer does not count; checked every 10 s), the relay turns off. The battery then only powers its backup port and stops charging or discharging to the house;
    • when HA reconnects, the relay turns back on, only if the failsafe turned it off. A global with restore_value remembers that across reboots; any relay turn-on clears it. A relay the owner switched off stays off;
    • relay_restore_mode: RESTORE_DEFAULT_ON: a reboot or OTA restores the last state, and ON when nothing was saved. A failsafe OFF is restored as OFF and switched on again when HA returns;
    • ESPHome's own api reboot_timeout (default 15 min) reboots the plug during a long HA outage. The relay stays off through it.

    It also trips on a Wi-Fi loss of 5 minutes. When the AC comes back the inverter goes back to Bypass, and Omnibattery may report backup_mode for a few minutes; the optimizer holds safe idle meanwhile.

    Flashing. Pushing changes only the ConfigMap that the ESPHome dashboard reads (Reloader restarts the esphome pod); the plug keeps its old firmware until it is flashed over OTA. Either use the dashboard at esphome.internal.grigri.cloud (smart-plug-v2-battery -> Install -> Wirelessly), or flash from apps/esphome/config (see its README.md):

    cd apps/esphome/config
make requirements
KUBE_CONTEXT=grigri make secrets
make smart-plug-v2-battery.yaml   # esphome run: compile + OTA

    The plug reboots once (a few seconds) and restores the relay ON. The failsafe firmware has been on the plug since 2026-11-03 (ESPHome 2026.10.1) and passed the hardware test on 2026-11-04. Flash while the battery is idle (direction switches off), then check switch.smart_plug_v2_battery is on.

    Known limit. A manual relay ON (HA or the plug's button) during a long HA absence is undone within 10 s: the absence counter is only reset by an HA reconnect or a reboot, not by a manual turn-on. To force AC while HA stays down, power-cycle the plug, then press the button (5 more minutes of grace). Fix candidate: let a manual turn-on suspend the failsafe until HA reconnects.

    Servers on the backup port. Since 2026-11-03 the backup port feeds the servers (~250 W) that host Home Assistant. A trip makes them run from the battery: at 50 % SOC that is roughly 8 h before the battery's own cutoff. An HA outage longer than that drains the battery and stops the servers; the plug then has to be switched on by hand (power-cycle + button).

    Failsafe test

    Passed on the flashed firmware on 2026-11-04 (see the result below). Repeat it after any change to the failsafe or the plug's firmware. It takes ~15 minutes and Home Assistant is really stopped for ~6 minutes. The servers switch to battery power for the switchover and back, the same switchover the commissioning test passed with the TV (max ping gap 0.22 s).

    Preconditions:

    • SOC above 30 %, battery in Bypass, Omnibattery balanced, backup-port load below the 500 W threshold.
    • Kill switch input_boolean.battery_optimizer_live off (both direction switches off), so the optimizer does not chase the battery while it is islanded. Note the time; turn it back on at the end.
    • A second way to reach the plug if HA misbehaves: the relay button, and the ESPHome logs from a workstation (apps/esphome/config: KUBE_CONTEXT=grigri make secrets, then make logs FILE=smart-plug-v2-battery.yaml; a log subscription is not a state subscriber, so it does not keep the relay on). Delete secrets.yaml afterwards.
    • The Kubernetes API must stay reachable from the workstation: the servers host HA and the cluster. Do not run it if a server reboot would be a problem.

    Steps (T = the moment HA is gone; the scaling only with the owner's explicit go):

    1. Start the ESPHome logs and note switch.smart_plug_v2_battery = on, sensor.venus_inverter_state = Bypass, sensor.venus_ac_offgrid_power ≈ servers' load.
    2. Stop HA for real, following Stopping Home Assistant under ArgoCD: scale argocd/argocd-repo-server to 0 first, then home-assistant/home-assistant-hass to 0. T: the pod is gone and the plug logs the lost API connection (Reading failed CONNECTION_CLOSED). Lighter alternative: Settings → Devices & services → ESPHome → smart-plug-v2-battery-9d9516 → ⋮ → Disable drops only the plug's API connection and leaves HA running.
    3. ~T+5:00: the log shows Battery failsafe: no Home Assistant for 5 min, relay OFF. Within seconds the battery reports Backup Mode (Omnibattery keeps polling it over Modbus) and the servers keep running (check the Kubernetes API or a ping).
    4. T+6: restore HA first, then argocd-repo-server (same doc). Within ~10 s of the HA API reconnect the log shows Battery failsafe: Home Assistant is back, relay ON; the switch is on in HA and the inverter returns to Bypass within 5–33 s. The home-assistant app shows sync Unknown until Argo's next refresh (about 3 min); a hard refresh clears it.
    5. Manual-off check: with HA connected, turn switch.smart_plug_v2_battery off, wait 30 s: it must stay off (the failsafe only restores what it turned off). Turn it on again; the battery returns to Bypass.
    6. Turn the kill switch back on and check the optimizer takes control at the next 15-minute slot.

    Pass: relay off at about T+5:00 (up to ~10 s early, see the result), back on within 20 s of the HA reconnect, manual off respected, servers never lost power. Fail or abort:

    • relay still on at T+6:00: the failsafe does not trip (likely api.connected still sees a state subscriber). Restore HA and treat the battery as unprotected against HA outages until fixed;
    • relay not back on 1 minute after HA is back: turn it on from HA; if HA cannot reach the plug, press the button once;
    • any server reboots during the switchover: put the servers back on mains and stop using the backup port until the switchover is understood.

    Rollback

    1. Turn input_boolean.battery_optimizer_live off. This is immediate: the service sets safe idle once and stops writing, and the watchdog repeats the safe idle in HA. In an emergency, also turn the Allow switches off on the Battery dashboard, or the battery's AC plug.

    How to read them

    1. Prometheus (prometheus-long-term) exports HA switch states (hass_switch_state), sensors and, verified 2026-11-06, the Omnibattery and Venus number entities: hass_number_state (unitless, such as kp and kd, e.g. hass_number_state{entity="number.omnibattery_pd_controller_kp"}) and the unit-suffixed hass_number_state_w, _s, _percent, _celsius, _kwh and _h.     2. select values and attributes (min/max): run a short Python inside the optimizer pod. It uses the pod's own HA token file (BATTERY_OPTIMIZER_HA_TOKEN_FILE) and URL (BATTERY_OPTIMIZER_HA_URL, a ws URL: the script converts it to http and strips /api/websocket) with GET /api/states. The token never leaves the pod and is never printed. Use a GET only, never a POST with it. Save the script as hastates.py and pipe it on stdin:
    kubectl --context=grigri -n battery-optimizer exec -i deploy/battery-optimizer-service -c service -- python - < hastates.py

    import os,json,urllib.request,re
tok=open(os.environ['BATTERY_OPTIMIZER_HA_TOKEN_FILE']).read().strip()
base=os.environ['BATTERY_OPTIMIZER_HA_URL'].rstrip('/')
base=re.sub(r'^ws',r'http',base); base=re.sub(r'/api/websocket$','',base)
req=urllib.request.Request(base+'/api/states',headers={'Authorization':'Bearer '+tok})
st=json.load(urllib.request.urlopen(req,timeout=20))
pat=re.compile(r'^(number|select|switch|sensor)\.(omnibattery|venus)_.*(discharg|max_charge|power_limit|force_mode|work_mode|user_mode|manual|contracted|pd_|max_power|backup|premium|rs485|allow|set_charge|set_discharge|ac_power|battery_power|status)')
for s in sorted(st,key=lambda s:s['entity_id']):
  if pat.search(s['entity_id']):

    Omnibattery PD controller settings log

    Policy (owner decision 2026-11-06). The optimizer analyses the PD response and recommends values. The owner sets them by hand in HA, and every change gets a row in the log below with date, old -> new, why and result. The optimizer does not write PD settings today. A later step may let it, which would need an ADR-0007 amendment in the app repo plus bounded allowlist entries and tests.

    How the settings act (integration source at commit 2c94da9, manifest 1.5.0):

    • Cycles run on each grid-sensor change plus a 2 s timer. Event-triggered cycles closer than pd_min_cycle_interval are dropped; the timer is not. The timer resets the throttle clock, so a dropped update waits up to about 2 s for the next cycle (see loop timing).
    • The grid is filtered with an EMA of tau 2 s, which snaps on jumps over 200 W.
    • Load-down guard (corrects the earlier text, 2026-11-07). A load-down is corrected by the _apply_discharge_ceiling guard (control/residual_load.py, Omnibattery 1.5.1b1), not by the feedforward. After the rate limiter the guard caps the discharge at grid + battery AC power - target, so a step is removed in one cycle. Three events matched its prediction within 1-2 W. kp, kd and max_power_change do not decide load-downs.
    • Nothing happens within deadband of the target.
    • Feedforward (not the load-down path, see the guard above): an error jump above max(5 x deadband, 400 W) that is confirmed on the next sample (80 %) issues one deadbeat command, bypassing the rate limit. It has a 10 s cooldown, and an opposite-sign step within 30 s is treated as a pulsing load.
    • Otherwise incremental PD: Kp is applied per 2 s, the derivative is filtered, and the change is clamped by max_power_change per 2 s.
    • direction_hysteresis gates charge/discharge flips.
    • Selecting a pd_tuning_profile overwrites only kp, kd and max_power_change. Profiles:
    Profile kp kd max_power_change (W)
    very_smooth 0.22 0.15 400
    smooth 0.30 0.25 600
    balanced 0.35 0.30 800
    aggressive 0.55 0.45 1200
    very_aggressive 0.75 0.45 2000

    Editing a number by hand makes the select show custom. To revert, write the numbers, not the profile. - relay_cooldown must stay 0: it holds discharge at 100 W or more after demand falls, which causes export.

    Loop timing and the grid meter (measured 2026-11-07).

    • A cycle runs on every Shelly update plus the 2 s timer, and updates inside pd_min_cycle_interval are dropped. Because the timer resets the throttle clock, a dropped update waits up to about 2 s: about 50% of updates at 1.0 s, about 25% at 0.5 s. The EMA snaps on jumps over 200 W.
    • Without the guard (battery AC power unreadable) cutting 1.4 kW takes 5-6 cycles, about 10-12 s.
    • Grid meter: Shelly EM Gen1 (SHEM, firmware v1.14.0). Mean update interval 1.69 s (2.0 s in 57% of intervals). On load drops it sees the change 0.3-2.0 s after the heat pump's own sensor (median about 0.95 s). Transport: the 2026-11-07 investigation read it as CoIoT UDP push to HA core, but the cutover record in Design notes has the grid power entity on MQTT every 2 s (or sooner on a change), which fits the 1.69 s mean better, and CoIoT multicast reaching HA core is not recorded anywhere. Unverified; check before relying on either.
    • Dropouts of 12-35 s happened about 20 times between 2026-11-03 and 11-05, none since the HA restart at 17:18 UTC on 11-05. During a dropout Omnibattery holds its last command.
