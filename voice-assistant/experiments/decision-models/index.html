    Decision models for Kivi: can a small classifier rescue the commands the built-in intents miss?

    Status: DONE for the synthetic corpus, the fake homes of kivi-fake-v1 (text) and the owner's real names (text, counts only); offline, measured on the workstation Date: 2026-11-05 Systems: workstation z800 (Ryzen 9 5950X 16 cores, RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB, shared with another GPU job during the runs); inferred for prusik (Zen 4 CPU, GTX 1060 3 GB Pascal sm_61) Related: small local models (corpus, transcripts, embedding router, GPU budget), conversation agent design, conversation agent evaluation (phrases, fake home, grader) Risk: offline. Home Assistant was read once, read-only (registry lists, exposure list and states over the websocket API; no service call, no conversation request). No cluster change, no TV, no Kiais request. Scripts: scripts/voice-assistant/decision_models/ on top of agent_eval/ and small_models/

    Evidence labels: documented (model card, package source, version named), measured (run on z800 in this study), inferred (computed from the above, not run on the target), hypothesis. All audio is synthetic (Piper voices, TV-content mixes); no real voice was used.

    Question

    Kivi's pipeline today: Whisper large-v3-turbo on the GTX 1060, then Home Assistant's built-in intents (prefer_local_intents, about 1 s end to end), and everything they do not answer goes to the Kiais LLM (about 4.1 s for a command, about 2.3 s for a general question; design note). Can a small non-generative decision model (picks one option per typed question with a probability, generates no text) sit between the two and execute the home commands the built-in intents miss, fast and without wrong actions, handing everything it is unsure about, and every general question, to Kiais?

    built-in intents miss -> decision stage: request type, action, area, device kind (one pass),
                         then which device if several match; our code parses the number
   |- every used answer at or above the threshold, and the combination is valid -> execute locally
   |- otherwise (unsure, general question, invalid combination)                    -> Kiais

    Summary

    1. Not worth wiring in as they are (measured). Used zero-shot (typed questions over the home's options, no training), no candidate rescues a useful share of the missed commands at a near-zero wrong-action rate. With the gate set so that wrong actions stay at about 1 % of what reaches the stage, the decision models execute correctly 0 to 8 % of the commands the built-in intents miss on the corpus transcripts, 4 to 6 % on the kivi-fake-v1 text, 3 to 6 % on its audio subset, and at most 1 of 27 on commands built from the owner's real names. Everything else still goes to Kiais.
    2. The embedding and fuzzy-matching baseline is as good or better on everything except the corpus: 11 % against 4 to 6 % on kivi-fake-v1 text (paired McNemar p < 1e-6), about the same rescues on audio. Its confidence is coarse, though: on audio it cannot be gated below 1.6 % wrong actions, because Whisper drops the question marks and state questions ("¿Está abierta la luz?") look like orders.
    3. Best decision model: Quyet-1.0-Tiny (183 M, Apache-2.0) with English instructions: 20 of 265 missed corpus commands (8 %, against 3 for the baseline, p < 0.001), 6 % on fake-v1 text and audio. Laya multilingual (322 M) is close to chance zero-shot on the action and area questions in Spanish, as its card warns ("not a zero-shot decision engine"); the English Laya root does not understand Spanish. Quyet-Small (328 M) is no better than Tiny.
    4. The confidence gate does not transfer: a threshold fitted on half of the phrases gives up to 17 wrong actions on the other half. Negatives are not the problem at those thresholds (TV dialogue with command verbs, general, live and chit-chat questions: none acted on in the text sets, 1 of 54 TV-dialogue transcripts on audio by Quyet-Tiny en); the problem is that a safe threshold hands off almost every command too.
    5. Speed and memory are not the obstacle: one decision of the Spanish-capable candidates takes 15 to 360 ms on 16 CPU threads and 17 to 80 ms on the workstation GPU (fp32). In fp32, only the baseline (0.75 GiB) and Quyet-Tiny (0.90 GiB, just) fit the 1.0 GiB left on the GTX 1060; Laya multilingual and Quyet-Small (1.5 GiB) do not. A CPU on prusik would do.

    Recommendation: do not add a zero-shot decision stage to Home Assistant. If the owner wants a local decider, it has to be trained on Spanish home commands (the decider PoC has the data, and custom sentences cover much of the same room more cheaply), then re-measured with this harness. The candidate to fine-tune is Quyet-Tiny, or a small classifier on the embedding model, run on the prusik CPU (no risk to Whisper's VRAM, about 0.15 s per decision) rather than on the 1060.

    The owner's description of Laya ("ModernBERT-large, about 421 M, multilingual") mixes two checkpoints: the 421 M root checkpoint is English-only by its card, the multilingual one is mmBERT-base with 322 M parameters. Both were run; the multilingual one is the relevant candidate.

    Card caveats that matter here (documented): Laya's base checkpoints are "near chance on typed-decisions zero-shot ... a fast base to specialise, not a zero-shot decision engine", they ship over-confident and laya-multilingual has no fitted temperatures (all 1.0 in its config); action.act_probability "carries no usable signal yet", so the gate uses the answer probability. Quyet ships fitted temperatures but names only English and Vietnamese as tuned languages.

    Code review before running (documented): both packages load only safetensors weights, JSON configs and a local tokenizer (snapshot_download restricted to those files; no repository .py, no trust_remote_code, no pickle). Quyet's manifest SHA-256 sums matched. The newest laya (0.4.24, uploaded the same morning) adds a dependency on the same author's new vegaml package, so the 19-day-old 0.4.9 without it was used; the card says the checkpoints did not change. On CUDA both packages autocast (Laya fp16 on pre-Ampere GPUs, Quyet bf16); for the GTX 1060 (Pascal sm_61: no bf16, very slow fp16) the evaluation turns autocast off and runs fp32 everywhere.

    Method

    • The decision stage (decide.py, identical for every candidate): one forward pass with five choice questions: request type (home command, timer, state question, anything else), action (ten options: on, off, set a value, volume up and down, mute, unmute, pause or stop, resume, next), timer action, area (every area of the home plus "no area named" and "the whole home") and device kind (lights, blinds, awnings, gates, climate, TV, speakers, fans, plugs, everything; only kinds the home has, each with up to six device names in its description). When the area and kind leave several devices, a second question asks which one (or all of them). Questions with more than ten options are split into knockout rounds. Our code parses the value (digits, Spanish number words, "y medio", "a la mitad", "al máximo") and the timer length, resolves the entities and maps the decision to a Home Assistant intent call. Invalid combinations (a volume action on a light, a set command without a number, a kind with no device in the area) hand off.
    • Gate: the minimum, over the questions the decision used, of the probability of the chosen option. Swept from 0 to 0.999. The chosen threshold is the lowest one whose wrong actions are at most 1 % of the items that reach the stage; it is fitted in-sample, so a phrase-split check (fit on half of the phrases, apply to the other half) is reported as well.
    • Instruction variants for the decision models: Spanish (es) or English (en) question and option texts (the state is always the Spanish transcript), and neutral option keys A, B, ... with the meaning only in the description (esN, enN; Laya's card suggests this when label keys dominate).
    • Outcome of an item that reaches the stage: rescued (the call matches the phrase's expectation from agent_eval: intent, exact target entities, value), wrong action (any executed call that does not, and any call on a general, live or state question, an unknown target or a two-action request), handed off to Kiais.
    • Test sets:
    • Corpus: Whisper large-v3-turbo transcripts of the 69 graded single-turn phrases of agent_eval spoken by the 5 intelligible Piper voices, with the verbs-only Whisper prompt (the shipped one), clean and mixed with TV content at +5 and 0 dB (run turbo_b5_vonly of the small local models study; 1035 transcripts). Each transcript first goes through the built-in matcher (agent_eval/builtin_intents.py, hassil 3.12.1 and home-assistant-intents 2026.10.6 as in Home Assistant 2026.10.4); only what it does not answer reaches the stage. Main subset: the graded home commands it misses (265); negatives: every general, live and state question (420). Sanity: the commands it answers correctly (319), run through the stage as if it came first.
    • kivi-fake-v1 (built by the decider proof-of-concept, read-only here): four fake homes, 17 phrasing styles (direct, polite, short, word order, disfluent, relative, Latin American, Spanish colloquial, implicit, multi-action, state, missing target, out of domain, general, live, chit-chat, TV dialogue with command verbs), template and validated LLM paraphrases, gold intent, entities and value; the text of all 2273 items and Whisper transcripts of a 500-item audio subset at three noise levels.
    • The owner's real names (private): the exposed entities, areas and aliases read once from Home Assistant (read-only), templated commands with gold targets generated from them (real_phrases.py, text only), plus the general and live questions of agent_eval. Only counts are published.
    • Paired tests: exact McNemar on the same items against the baseline (rescued, and wrong actions). Items of the same phrase in different voices or noise levels are not independent, so p-values are indicative.
    • Latency: GPU fp32 on the RTX 4060 Ti with decision_eval.py bench (warm, one process per candidate, 40 distinct clean transcripts twice; the card was shared with another job at 100 % utilisation, so an upper bound). CPU fp32 on 16 threads: the per-decision times of the corpus accuracy run (Ryzen 9 5950X, load average about 8 to 25 from other jobs). Peak VRAM: torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated and the process's own nvidia-smi figure (includes the CUDA context).

The baseline cannot be gated below 1.6 % wrong actions on audio: Whisper drops the question marks, so "¿Está abierta la luz de la terraza?" arrives as "Está abierta la luz de la terraza." and the 3-NN votes "turn on" unanimously (10 of its 15 negatives acted on are such state questions; 3 are two-action requests of which it executes the first, 2 name a device the area does not have). Phrase-split check: Quyet-Tiny en 36 rescued / 1 wrong on one half, 11 / 7 on the other; the baseline 35 / 16 and 28 / 7.

    3. The owner's real names (text, counts only), measured

    Home Assistant exposes 100 entities to Assist in 5 areas (50 switches, 25 of them without an area; 25 sensors; 5 climate, 4 media players, 6 buttons, 3 lights, 3 covers, 2 fans, 1 vacuum, 1 binary sensor; no entity aliases, Spanish aliases on the areas). The first-pass questions then have 7 areas and 7 device kinds, and "which plug" has up to 50 options (knockout rounds of at most 10).     With the real home the decision models rescue almost nothing even ungated. The area names are English labels with Spanish aliases, the 67 actionable entity names are all distinct but long (median five words), and the "which device" question over dozens of plugs goes through knockout rounds whose confidences multiply. Of Quyet-Tiny en's ungated failures, most are a wrong device kind or area (8 "no target", 4 media actions on a non-media kind) or a wrong cover action (5 wrong calls on "para ..." and "... a la mitad").     4. Latency and memory

    One decision on the fake home is 1.0 to 1.4 forward calls on average (the "which device" question adds a second call when area and kind leave several devices).     On the real home the option lists are longer (up to 50 plugs, names of about five words). That was not measured cleanly (the workstation was saturated by other jobs from about 17:50 on). In one contended GPU run, Quyet-Tiny took 206 ms p50 per decision on the real home's lists and the baseline 119 ms in the same conditions, against 26 and 17 ms on the fake home at low load. The longer lists therefore cost at most a modest factor on top of the contention (inferred). Quyet-Tiny's peak process VRAM with the real lists was 926 MiB (measured).

    GPU figures on the 4060 Ti are upper bounds (another job kept it at 100 % utilisation). The GTX 1060 column scales them by 4.5, the ratio measured for Whisper's encoder between the two cards (small local models, section 2); it is an inference, not a measurement. VRAM is the process's own nvidia-smi figure on the 4060 Ti (weights, activations and the CUDA context; the tensors alone in brackets); on the 1060 the weights and activations are the same size in fp32, the context size may differ. Only the baseline and Quyet-Tiny fit the 1.0 GiB budget, and Quyet-Tiny only just. Laya multilingual and Quyet-Small would need fp16 storage (very slow on Pascal) or the CPU. On prusik's Zen 4 CPU the CPU columns should be similar or faster (inferred, as in the earlier study).

    Against the alternative, every candidate is fast enough: even 0.5 s on a CPU is far below the 3 s the Kiais round trip costs for a command. Latency is not what rules them out; accuracy is.

    Conclusion and recommendation

    • Zero-shot decision models do not earn a place between the built-in intents and Kiais (confidence high for zero-shot use on this kind of data: three test sets, two model families, four instruction variants, and the per-question accuracy shows why: the action and area questions are near chance in Spanish). What they would save is small: with the PoC's bound of about 3 s saved per rescued command, rescuing 6 % of the missed commands saves about 0.2 s per missed command on average, at a wrong-action rate of about 1 % of what reaches the stage.
    • The baseline (no new model) is a fair comparison and often better, but its confidence is too coarse to gate safely, and it acts on state questions once Whisper has dropped the question marks. Not recommended either, as it stands.
    • Where it would run, if a trained version is ever built: the prusik CPU (Quyet-Tiny about 0.15 s per decision on 16 workstation threads, more knockout rounds with many plugs), not the 1060: only Quyet-Tiny and the baseline fit its 1.0 GiB in fp32, and fp16 is slow on Pascal.
    • Wiring (unchanged from the design note): Home Assistant has one agent per pipeline, so a stage like this would be a small custom conversation agent that calls the intents itself and forwards the rest to Kiais with conversation.async_converse. Not proposed now.

    Rollback and cleanup

    Nothing changed in the cluster or Home Assistant. Home Assistant was read once over the websocket API with the Whisper name-fetch token (from its Kubernetes Secret into a 0600 file in memory, shredded afterwards); only list and get_states commands were sent. The real inventory and the phrases generated from it stay in the private directory. Locally, the uv package cache was deleted; to rerun, the pinned model snapshots (3.9 GB, Hugging Face cache layout) and the Python environment stay in the workstation's scratch work directory on the NVMe disk (about 9 GB), not under /home.

    Open questions

    • Fine-tuning: both model cards say the capability comes from fine-tuning on the domain (Laya's fine-tuned checkpoint goes from 0.36 to 0.77 on its own benchmark). The decider PoC has the data (kivi-fake-v1 train split) to test whether a fine-tuned Quyet-Tiny or Laya (or a small classifier on the embedding model) closes the gap; that is a separate, larger experiment.
    • Confidence: a gate on raw answer probabilities did not transfer between phrase sets. A fine-tuned model would need temperatures fitted on held-out data of the household's own phrasing.
    • Question marks: Whisper's transcripts lose "¿...?", so a stage behind STT cannot tell "¿Está encendida la luz?" from an order by punctuation; any stage needs state questions in its training or negatives.
    • Real voices and the real phrasing of the household (workstream c of the plan) decide how much room is left after custom sentences; all audio here is synthetic.
