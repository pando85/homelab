    Small local models for Kivi: GPU budget, router, STT corrector, local agent, and non-LLM fixes

    Status: DONE for the PC-only measurements; the real GTX 1060 measurement needs the owner's approval (proposal in the design note) Date: 2026-11-05 Systems: homelab node prusik (GTX 1060 3 GB, Pascal sm_61) read-only; measurements on the workstation z800 (RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB, Ryzen 9 5950X 16 cores / 32 threads, DDR4) Related: conversation agent evaluation (Kiais metrics and the harness reused here), STT hint list (uses the prompt numbers of section 5), satellite-side backlog section 12 (formerly EXP-019 of that repository) Risk: read-only on the cluster and Home Assistant; no TV, no sound, no microphone. Nothing was pulled into the cluster Ollama. Kiais received no requests from this study. Scripts: scripts/voice-assistant/small_models/ (this study) on top of scripts/voice-assistant/agent_eval/ (phrases, fake home, built-in intent matcher, chat harness)

    Evidence labels: documented (read in source, a manifest or a model card, version named), observed (read from the cluster, Prometheus or Loki on the date above), measured (run on z800 in this study), inferred (computed from the above, not run on the target), hypothesis.

    Question

    Can a small model on the cluster's GTX 1060 (or on a node CPU) make the Spanish pipeline "Kiwi" better, as a router (home command or general question), as a corrector of Whisper transcripts, or as the home-control agent itself, and how does that compare with non-LLM fixes (Whisper prompt, model size, Home Assistant's built-in intents)? Owner's target: commands handled locally, fast and accurate; general and live questions routed to the Kiais gateway.

    Summary

    1. VRAM budget (observed): Whisper large-v3-turbo int8 holds 1140 MiB on the 1060 and peaks at 1556 MiB while transcribing; Jellyfin may also use the GPU. The owner set the LLM budget to 1.0 GiB, guarded so it can never take Whisper's burst.
    2. No model that fits 1.0 GiB is a usable Home Assistant agent (measured). Nine models of 270 M to 1.5 B parameters passed 0 to 16 % of the control phrases with Home Assistant's tool schema; the first usable size is qwen3:4b-instruct (65 % of all control phrases, and 10 of 13 of the phrases the built-in intents miss, against 11 of 13 for Kiais fast). It needs about 2.5 GB plus cache, so on this cluster it would run on the prusik CPU or split CPU/GPU.
    3. The router does not need an LLM. Home Assistant's own strict matching (prefer_local_intents) already sends every general and live question away (21 of 21) and keeps 32 of 47 home phrases local. A multilingual sentence-embedding model (278 M) with 3 nearest neighbours, behind the built-in match, reaches 66 of 68 (97 %) on the reference texts in tens of milliseconds on a CPU. Small LLM routers were worse (section 6).
    4. The Whisper prompt is the cheapest accuracy gain (measured): area and device names as initial_prompt raised built-in intent success from 65 % to 70 % at +5 dB of TV content (no gain on clean audio); the generic command verbs, with or without the names, raised it to 85 to 88 % clean, 74 to 75 % at +5 dB and 57 % at 0 dB (from 82, 65 and 46 %; verbs only: 52 phrases fixed and 3 broken over the four conditions). A static verbs-only prompt works on the deployed image today. hotwords gave output identical to initial_prompt. Whisper small and medium were significantly worse than the deployed large-v3-turbo, and large-v3 was not better.
    5. LLM transcript correctors do not pay off (measured): every model that fits broke about as many commands as it fixed, or more, and some rewrote a command to a different device; the only clearly positive one (qwen2.5:3b, 1.9 GB) gained less than the Whisper prompt.
    6. Latency on the 1060 (observed from its logs): Whisper takes 0.78 s plus 0.03 s per second of audio (p50 0.86 s), almost independent of the command length, because the encoder always runs a 30 s window.

    Budget (inferred): 3072 MiB minus Whisper's peak (1556 MiB) leaves 1516 MiB. A Jellyfin transcode reserves roughly 250 to 300 MiB (hypothesis, not observed here), and a CUDA context of a second process costs roughly 100 to 150 MiB on Pascal (hypothesis), so about 1.0 GiB remains for an LLM's weights, KV cache and compute buffers. The owner confirmed 1.0 GiB with Jellyfin coexisting.

    Whisper large-v3-turbo is not the "small model": it is the second-largest Spanish-capable Whisper and already takes about half of the card. CTranslate2 runs int8 on sm_61 as int8_float32 (documented, CTranslate2 quantization table).

    A risk of the current setup (inferred): Ollama sizes its offload against the free VRAM when it loads a model. While Whisper is idle it sees about 1.86 GiB free and can take the 416 MiB that Whisper needs during a transcription, after which Whisper's CUDA allocation fails. The guard is OLLAMA_GPU_OVERHEAD plus a context cap (design note).

    2. Whisper latency on the 1060 (observed from the Whisper pod's own logs)

    wyoming-faster-whisper logs Processing audio with duration ... before and the transcript after each request at INFO level (documented, handler.py of 2.4.0), so Loki holds a timing per request. Over 2026-10-16 to 2026-11-05, 445 requests (the transcripts themselves are private and were not copied into this repository):

    Audio length n STT p50 / p90 (s)
    under 2 s 132 0.84 / 0.89
    2 to 3 s 104 0.85 / 0.90
    3 to 4 s 74 0.85 / 0.91
    4 to 6 s 35 0.88 / 0.96
    6 to 10 s 73 0.93 / 0.99
    over 10 s 25 0.89 / 1.06

    Least squares: STT = 0.78 s + 0.029 s per second of audio. The fixed part is the encoder over a 30 s window. The same model and settings take 0.19 s per item on the 4060 Ti (measured), so the 1060 is about 4.5 times slower on this compute-bound work; this factor is used below for prefill estimates (inferred). Decoding of an LLM is memory-bound and scales with bandwidth (192 against 288 GB/s, about 1.5 times; inferred).

    3. Method

    • Phrases and fake home: agent_eval/phrases_es.yaml (44 home control, 15 general, 11 live information, 6 two-turn follow-ups) and agent_eval/inventory_es.yaml (a fake Spanish home). No real data.
    • Built-in intents: agent_eval/builtin_intents.py runs hassil 3.12.1 with home-assistant-intents 2026.10.6 (the pins of HA 2026.10.4) with the default agent's slot lists and the satellite area as context; inferred, not a run of Home Assistant. Of the 44 control reference texts, 29 resolve to the expected call; 15 fall through (5 because Home Assistant hands HassMediaSearchAndPlay to an LLM agent with control, 9 without a match, 1 with no tool twin).
    • Semi-synthetic audio (small_models/stt_corpus.py): the 70 single-turn phrases spoken by the seven Spanish Piper voices of the satellite project's Phase 2 corpus, at -22 dBFS active speech, clean and mixed with random excerpts of its TV content set (Spanish narration, English film dialogue, songs with vocals) at +10, +5 and 0 dB speech-to-content ratio: 1960 clips. The two es_ES-mls_*-low voices are unintelligible even digitally (also found in the satellite's Phase 2 session 2) and are excluded from every figure; 5 voices remain (2 ES male and female, 1 ES male, 2 MX), 350 clips per condition. No room acoustics or echo-canceller residue are modelled.
    • Transcription (small_models/stt_eval.py): faster-whisper 1.2.1 with the call of wyoming-faster-whisper 2.4.0 (language es, beam 5, optional initial_prompt; no VAD, no hotwords) on the 4060 Ti. Scored by WER (the satellite's Phase 2 normalisation v1.1 with numerals spelled on both sides) and by built-in intent success: the share of the 29 handleable control phrases whose transcript still resolves to the right call. Paired differences use the exact McNemar test; items of the same phrase in different voices are not independent, so p-values are indicative.
    • Router (small_models/router_eval.py), corrector (small_models/corrector_eval.py) and agent (agent_eval/run.py against a private Ollama 0.35.1 bound to 127.0.0.1:11500, models from the Ollama library at their default quantisation, num_ctx 8192 as Home Assistant's Ollama integration sets by default, thinking off).
    • Latency: every timing excludes the one-off model load (a warm-up request first; the model store was on a hard disk). The workstation was shared with a wake-word training job (load average 25 to 98) and the GPU with the other runs of this study, so accuracy figures are clean but the timing figures of the routers, corrector and agent runs are upper bounds; the dedicated speed benchmarks are in section 9.

    The Home Assistant style system prompt plus the 26 tool definitions of the fake home is 3633 tokens (measured with qwen2.5:1.5b's tokenizer), so a tool agent needs num_ctx of at least 4096, and Home Assistant's Ollama integration uses 8192 by default (documented). A real home with more exposed entities needs more.

    Runtimes (documented unless marked): llama.cpp (the engine inside Ollama) supports sm_61 with integer dp4a kernels and partial offload (-ngl) and AVX-512 on Zen 4; Ollama adds model management and the API Home Assistant's ollama integration speaks; Home Assistant 2026.10.4 also has a core llama_cpp integration (base URL of a llama-server) and a core litellm integration (URL plus key, OpenAI client) (documented, config_flow.py at tag 2026.10.4). CTranslate2 already runs Whisper; its LLM support is narrower (hypothesis that it brings nothing over llama.cpp here). ExLlamaV2, vLLM and TensorRT-LLM rely on FP16 tensor maths; Pascal consumer cards run FP16 at a small fraction of FP32 speed and lack tensor cores, so they are not realistic on the 1060 (inferred). ONNX Runtime GenAI could run small models on CPU (hypothesis; not measured).

Beam 1 against beam 5: pooled noisy 4 / 16 (worse). The names prompt was "Kivi, asistente de casa. " followed by the 32 distinct area and device names and aliases of the fake home (about 80 tokens); it made Whisper write "Kivi" into 3 of 1400 transcripts whose audio has no "Kivi" (keep the assistant's name out of the prompt). hotwords in faster-whisper fills the same prompt slot, which explains the identical output.

    What still fails with the prompt on clean audio is mostly the command verb at the start of a short utterance ("Reanuda la tele" heard as "Renuda la tele", "Apaga la tele" as "Hasta acá la tele", "Pausa la tele" as "Pops a la tele", "Apaga la luz" as "Para la luz") and words outside the names list ("temporizador" as "Tiempoizador", "toldo" as "tolo"). Names alone cannot fix verbs, so a third variant appends the generic command vocabulary of Home Assistant's Spanish intents ("Enciende, apaga, sube, baja, abre, cierra, para, pausa, reanuda, silencia, quita el silencio, pon, temporizador, minutos, volumen, brillo, por ciento, grados.", no test sentence, about 40 more tokens; VERBS_ES in stt_eval.py). The rows in bold, with and without the names, are the largest gain of the study. Caveat: the verbs are the same generic verbs the test phrases use, as real commands do; the gain on the owner's own phrasing is to be confirmed with real transcripts (hint-list phase 2).

    Verbs only against no prompt: clean 9 / 0 (p 0.004), +10 dB 11 / 1 (p 0.006), +5 dB 15 / 1 (p 0.001), 0 dB 17 / 1 (p < 0.001). Verbs first then names against no prompt: 4 / 1, 10 / 2, 14 / 1, 16 / 3. Against names then verbs, neither variant differs significantly (p 0.06 to 1.0). So on this fake home the verbs carry the gain; the names add nothing measurable, probably because the fake home's names are common Spanish words that Whisper already knows (real names may differ). Neither verbs variant leaked "Kivi" (0 of 1400 each).

    For the hint list: put the command verbs in the prompt, not only names; a static verbs-only --initial-prompt already works on the deployed 2.4.0 image and needs no home data; the 3.8.1 order (verbs first, then the names) is as good. Keep "Kivi" out (it leaked into 3 of 1400 transcripts in every run whose prompt started with it); the prompt's latency cost is in section 9.

    6. Router

    Task: label a transcript "casa" (handled in the house: built-in intents or a local agent) or "kiais" (general knowledge, chat, open-world live information). Labels: all 44 control phrases plus "¿Qué tiempo hace fuera?", "¿Qué hora es?" and "¿Qué día es hoy?" are "casa" (47); the 15 general and the other live phrases are "kiais" (21); the two calendar phrases are not scored. Methods:

    • builtin: "casa" when Home Assistant's strict built-in matching resolves an intent (what prefer_local_intents does; HassMediaSearchAndPlay and HassGetState count as not matched, as Home Assistant hands them to an LLM agent with control).
    • embedding: granite-embedding:278m or paraphrase-multilingual (both multilingual, Apache-2.0), nearest class centroid or 3 nearest neighbours over 80 synthetic training phrases written for this study (40 per class, no sentence shared with the test set; in router_eval.py).
    • LLM: JSON-schema constrained {"ruta": "casa" | "kiais"} with a Spanish instruction, the area and device names and four few-shot pairs; about 400 prompt tokens.
    • builtin + X: built-in match first, X only for the rest (how X would sit behind prefer_local_intents).

    Hybrid CPU and 1060 (inferred)

    With 1.0 GiB of the 1060, about 11 of the 36 layers of qwen3:4b-instruct Q4_K_M (2.3 GiB of weights) fit next to their share of the KV cache and the compute buffers. Generation time per token is roughly the sum over layers of bytes over bandwidth: about 70 % of the weights still come from system memory, so generation would be about 20 % faster than CPU-only, and prompt processing gains a little more. That does not justify the risk to Whisper's transcription peak.

    Conclusion and confidence

    • No small model on the 1060 earns its VRAM. As the home agent the models that fit fail (0 to 16 %); as a router an embedding model on the CPU is as good and faster; as an STT corrector they break as much as they fix. Confidence high for this fake home and tool schema (the failure modes are structural: slot over-filling and no tool call), medium for other homes.
    • The best accuracy gain is the Whisper prompt with the generic command verbs (names added nothing measurable on this fake home; a static verbs-only prompt works on the deployed image). Measured on synthetic voices and TV-content mixes: confidence medium that the size of the gain carries over to real voices in the room, high that it does not cost latency.
    • A local agent needs at least about 4 B parameters (qwen3:4b-instruct), which on this cluster means the prusik CPU: about as accurate and about as fast as Kiais fast for the commands the built-in intents miss, with a cold-start cost of tens of seconds (inferred from the workstation; the owner-approval experiment in the design note would measure it).
    • The ranked options and the proposed GitOps change are in the design note.

    Open questions

    • The fake home's names were chosen to be natural; the owner's real names, aliases and exposure decide the real built-in hit rate. Recheck with the owner's phrases against Home Assistant's read-only recognition endpoint.
    • Real voices in the room (reverberation, echo-canceller residue, distance) are not modelled; the satellite project's real-voice set would confirm the Whisper prompt gain.
    • prusik's real CPU speed for qwen3:4b-instruct and the cold-start time (owner-approval experiment in the design note).
    • Whether a router should err towards the local agent or towards Kiais depends on whether a local agent is added at all.
